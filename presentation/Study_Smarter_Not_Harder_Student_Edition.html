Act 1 does studying pay?

Do students who study more get better grades?

+2.4

points out of 20, from under 2 h to 5-10 h a week. That is about three-quarters of the usual spread in grades (3.2 points).

Past 10 hours a week, no extra gain. Few students study that much, so treat this end with care.

The story so far, all six parts
    Back to the opening vote: 0 for The Grinder, 0 for The Regular. The data credit both studying and showing up, and the "perfect attendance" record is exactly where the zero grades were hiding.
    For you: your last grade predicts your next, so change course early.

    The three numbers behind the verdict

    Effort
    +2.38 raw (<2 h → 5-10 h); +0.42 per step after controls (M3, p = 0.001)
    History
    R² 0.34 → 0.86 when G1, G2 join the model
    Zeros
    15 of 15 zero grades with 0 absences; without them, M3 log(abs+1) = −0.45
    What we can't claim

    What can't this study prove?

    1. Patterns, not proof.Students choose how much to study, so keen students may differ in ways we can't see.
    2. Earlier grades blur the effort effect.Those grades were partly earned by studying, so the last model undersells effort.
    3. "Apparent dropouts" is our reading.The data never say why a grade is 0, so every key result is shown with and without them.
    4. Rough measures.Study time is four tick-boxes, and grades are capped at 0-20.
    5. One place, one year.Two Portuguese schools in 2005-06, with few students in the top study group.
